## Long Description
Originally developed at MIT by Harold Abelson and Gerald Jay Sussman, *Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs* has taught the fundamental ideas of computation to generations of programmers.

This course adapts the core SICP curriculum into Python, following the approach pioneered by UC Berkeley's CS 61A. Instead of Scheme, all examples and exercises use Python—but the deep ideas remain the same.

**What you'll learn:**
- Higher-order functions and closures
- Environment diagrams and name resolution
- Recursive thinking and process analysis
- Data abstraction and abstraction barriers
- Sequences, trees, and recursive data structures
- Mutation, state, and identity
- Object-oriented programming from first principles
- Building a working interpreter

**Who this is for:**
- Self-taught programmers wanting CS fundamentals
- Bootcamp graduates seeking deeper understanding
- CS students preparing for advanced courses
- Experienced developers revisiting the classics
